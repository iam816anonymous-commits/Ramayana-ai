Bal Kanda: The Story of Rama's Birth and Childhood
The Bal Kanda describes the birth of Rama to King Dasharatha and Queen Kausalya in Ayodhya. Dasharatha, the king of Kosala, had three wives but no children. After performing a sacred sacrifice (yagna), he was blessed with four sons: Rama, Bharata, and the twins Lakshmana and Shatrughna.
Rama and Lakshmana were mentored by the sage Vishwamitra, who took them to protect his forest hermitage from demons. Rama successfully defeated the demoness Tataka and later protected the sage's sacrifice from Maricha and Subahu.
During their journey, Vishwamitra took them to Mithila, where Rama won the hand of Sita, the daughter of King Janaka, by lifting and breaking the heavy bow of Lord Shiva.

Ayodhya Kanda: The Exile of Rama
The Ayodhya Kanda begins with King Dasharatha's decision to crown Rama as the crown prince. However, Queen Kaikeyi, influenced by her maid Manthara, demanded two boons that the king had promised her long ago. She asked for her son Bharata to be crowned king and for Rama to be exiled to the Dandaka forest for fourteen years.
Rama, ever dutiful and committed to dharma (righteousness), accepted the exile without hesitation to honor his father's word. Sita and Lakshmana insisted on accompanying him. The trio crossed the Ganges and entered the forest, leaving the city of Ayodhya in deep mourning. King Dasharatha, heartbroken by the separation from his beloved son, soon passed away.
Bharata, who was away during these events, returned to Ayodhya and was horrified by his mother's actions. He refused to take the throne and went to the forest to find Rama, begging him to return. Rama refused, insisting on fulfilling his father's promise. Bharata eventually returned with Rama's sandals, placing them on the throne as a symbol of Rama's authority while he ruled as a regent.
